## Learning goals

1. Understand the relationship among key ingredients of biosecurity: prevention; detection; response; attribution and deterrence.
2. Understand similarities and differences between biological, chemical, and nuclear weapons, including the concept of "reload."
3. Be aware of the list of "dual use" "deadly sins" and their recent update by the Trump Administration.

## Before class

### Read and watch

1. **[Glossary of Biosecurity Terms](https://biosecurityhandbook.com/appendices/glossary.html)**. Brian Tegemoh, *Biosecurity Handbook*, Appendix C.
   - Have a look and check out some terms that are unfamiliar to you.
2. **[Catastrophic Bioterrorism: What Is to Be Done?](https://ndupress.ndu.edu/Portals/68/Documents/occasional/CTNSP/Catastrophic-Bioterrorism.pdf)** Richard Danzig. (2 pages)
   - Read Section 1, pp. 1–2 carefully; peruse the rest optionally.
3. **[The National Blueprint for Biodefense](https://biodefensecommission.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/National-Blueprint-for-Biodefense-2024_final_.pdf)**. Bipartisan Commission on Biodefense, 2024. (25 pages)
   - Read pp. 7–31. Reading all 25 pages is preferred, but an AI summary might suffice.
4. **[What is Biosecurity: Explained](https://www.nti.org/risky-business/what-is-biosecurity-explained/)**. NTI bio webpage. (2 pages)
   - Read the whole thing.
5. **[Recorded lecture: dual use and the seven deadly sins](https://canvas.stanford.edu/courses/231306/files/folder/Videos%20to%20watch%20before%20class?preview=17607727)** (Canvas). (5 min video)
   - Watch the whole thing.
