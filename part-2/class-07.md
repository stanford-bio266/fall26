## Learning goals

1. Understand the value and limitations of vaccines in pandemic response.
2. Understand the key steps of vaccine development and approval, why this has historically taken a long time, and why COVID-19 vaccines rolled out (in some countries) in under a year; also current efforts to speed this further and challenges to equitable global distribution.
3. Understand the state of vaccine preparedness against a set of high-risk potential pandemic pathogens.
4. Anticipate what will and won't be known about vaccines at each stage of emergency deployment, and how that affects their use.
5. Make evidence-based prioritization of vaccines within and between populations amid this uncertainty.

2. **[Individuals cannot rely on COVID-19 herd immunity: Durable immunity to viral disease is limited to viruses with obligate viremic spread](https://journals.plos.org/plospathogens/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.ppat.1009509&type=printable)**. Yewdell JW. *PLoS Pathog* 2021;17(4):e1009509. (2 pages)
3. **[COVID-19 vaccines and herd immunity](https://web.archive.org/web/20201218055504/https://ccdd.hsph.harvard.edu/2020/12/17/covid-19-vaccines-and-herd-immunity/)**. Lipsitch M. Blog post, December 2020. (2 pages)

### Optional, for particular interests

1. **[Emergency Use Authorization for Vaccines Explained](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/vaccines/emergency-use-authorization-vaccines-explained)**. FDA.
   - For those interested in regulation/policy. EUAs made some people very nervous during COVID-19. This explanation is not all that clear, but it is official.
2. **[Model-informed COVID-19 vaccine prioritization strategies by age and serostatus](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abe6959)**. Bubar KM et al. *Science* 2021;371(6532):916–921.
   - The Class 5 "robustness" reading: an example of the process required for every allocation decision while vaccines are limited. Covered in lecture; of interest for the details.
3. **[Fairness and efficiency considerations in COVID-19 vaccine allocation strategies: A case study comparing front-line workers and 65–74 year olds in the United States](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36962865/)**. Rumpler E, Feldman JM, Bassett MT, Lipsitch M. *PLOS Glob Public Health* 2023;3(2):e0001378.
   - For those with an ethics/equity/policy bent. A real question faced by the Massachusetts advisory group on vaccine allocation, with a perhaps counterintuitive answer: an example of how quantitative considerations can enhance ethical deliberation.
