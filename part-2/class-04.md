## Learning goals

1. Recognize, critically evaluate, and use concepts from epidemiological modeling in the context of pandemics.
2. Understand how prevention and mitigation depend on phase, with different goals at different phases.
3. Appreciate how communication style can influence a population's response to pandemic interventions.
4. Discuss and form a view of the evidence base for pandemic interventions and the presence of irreducible uncertainty.
5. Define potential costs and benefits of pandemic interventions and discuss how they can be weighed.
6. Apply the concept of off-ramps and regular decision points to pandemic decision making.

## In class

1. Intro to this section of the course; form groups for the next class's readings
2. Questions and discussion about the video mini-lecture
3. Rotating small-group discussions drawing on readings and experience:
   - Lessons observed from COVID-19 in six areas. Group 1: communication and collateral damage. Group 2: decision making under uncertainty and individual leadership. Group 3: scientific preparedness and government powers/structures.
   - Rotate: is the US / the world more or less prepared on each dimension?
   - Rotate: propose one "peacetime" action that would help us do better next time.
4. Summary of learning goals and findings
