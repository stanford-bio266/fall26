## Class 17 learning goals

1. To know the definitions of mirror biological molecules, mirror organisms, and the current state of technology for each
2. To evaluate the potential benefits of mirror molecules and mirror organisms
3. To evaluate the potential risks of each.
4. To compare the regulation or restrictions on mirror life to those on dGOF in virology
5. To assess the relative importance of the different restrictions on scientific pursuit and the magnitude of biosecurity restrictions on these.

## Class 17 advance preparation

1. Read Rohden F, Hoheisel JD, Wieden, H-J (2021). **[Through the looking glass: Milestones on the road towards mirroring life](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tibs.2021.06.006)**. Trends in Biochemical Sciences, 46(11), 931–943. (10 pages)
2. Read K Adamala et al. **[Confronting risks of mirror life](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.ads9158)**. Science 386,1351-1353(2024). (3 pages)
3. Read Responsible research in synthetic biology and mirror-image molecular biology (2025). [https://responsiblesynbio.org/](https://responsiblesynbio.org/) (2 pages)
4. Read Zhu T. **[Mirror of the unknown: should research on mirror-image molecular biology be stopped?](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-02912-0)**. Nature. 2025 Sep 18;645(8081):588-91. (4 pages)
5. Post on Canvas. Is action needed now to restrict mirror life?

## Class 17 agenda

Discussion on Mirror life – what are the issues, risks, benefits? Differences and similarities with dGOF

What are the limitations on science? What are their relative magnitude in terms of promising ideas blocked? Are biosecurity-related restrictions major, minor, or example-dependent?
