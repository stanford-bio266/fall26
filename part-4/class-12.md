## Class 12 learning goals

1. To know and consider the strengths and weaknesses of the key ideas underlying the idea of unfettered, investigator-driven science as a key to innovation.
2. To identify some of the most important practical advances that have come from basic science roots.
3. To discuss the applicability of these principles to solving biosecurity challenges.
4. To consider how biosecurity restrictions may collide with these principles and how the conflict may be managed
5. To contrast the “blue sky” view with the notion of Pasteur’s Quadrant.

## Class 12 advance preparation

1. Classic texts about “blue sky” science
   - a. Read Bush V. **[Science: the endless frontier](https://nsf-gov-resources.nsf.gov/2023-04/EndlessFrontier75th_w.pdf)**. 1945. Summary, Chapters 1-2 (pp. 1-14 in the original report), beginning of Chapter 6 (pp. 33-35 in the original) (total of 17 pages)
   - b. Read Polanyi, M. 1962. **[THE REPUBLIC OF SCIENCE: Its Political and Economic Theory](http://www.jstor.org/stable/41821550)**. Minerva, 1(1), 54–73. Read pages 54-58. (5 pages)
2. Examples of how basic science feeds applications
   - a. Read Spector JM, Harrison RS, Fishman MC. **[Fundamental science behind today's important medicines](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/scitranslmed.aaq1787)**. Sci Transl Med. 2018 Apr 25;10(438):eaaq1787. doi: 10.1126/scitranslmed.aaq1787. PMID: 29695453.
   - b. Do one of the following:
     - i. Read Roberts RJ. **[How restriction enzymes became the workhorses of molecular biology.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1087929/)** Proc Natl Acad Sci U S A. 2005 Apr 26;102(17):5905-8. doi: 10.1073/pnas.0500923102. Epub 2005 Apr 19. PMID: 15840723; PMCID: PMC1087929. (written by a Nobel Prize winner but less scientific content and a little dull, 4 pages) OR
     - ii. Watch **[The Scissor Servant: Restriction Enzymes and the Birth of Genetic Engineering](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4JDSg9I_aCs)**. Youtube channel: The Genetic Basis of Stuff and Things. 2022 (chatty and cartoonish, but scientifically accurate and more science content)
3. An update to the classic approach: the idea of Pasteur’s Quadrant. Read Crawford J. 2020. **[Pasteur’s Quadrant](https://blog.rootsofprogress.org/pasteurs-quadrant)** blog post, Roots of Progress blog (3 pages). OPTIONAL for a deeper dive and a riff: Pettigrew RI, Cooke JP. **[At the nexus of science, engineering, and medicine: Pasteur's quadrant reconsidered.](https://academic.oup.com/pnasnexus/article-lookup/doi/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgac092)** PNAS Nexus. 2022;1(3).
4. Post on Canvas about what all this reading might have to do with biosecurity.
