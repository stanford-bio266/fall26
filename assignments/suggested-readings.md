## Part 1

- Respond to the remainder of Jacobsen, [*Biological War: A Scenario*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/783250/biological-war-by-annie-jacobsen/) (Parts 3 to end).
- Analyze the data the CDC provided during the pandemic: what are the strengths and weaknesses of this presentation for ordinary citizens? Use the [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/web/20201017044641/https://covid.cdc.gov/covid-data-tracker/#cases_casesinlast7days) for a date around October 2020 and navigate to [covid.cdc.gov/covid-data-tracker](https://covid.cdc.gov/covid-data-tracker/).
- Respond to one or more posts on [Inspired Air](https://inspiredair.substack.com/).

## Parts 1 & 2

- Assess the potential benefits and limitations for biosecurity of new pathogen-agnostic pharmaceutical interventions that target innate immunity. References: [Nature Reviews Immunology](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41577-024-00990-1) and [Science](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aea1260).

## Part 2

- Identify media clips (e.g. videos) of COVID-era pandemic communication from two or more jurisdictions (US states, countries, WHO). Compare their style, approach to uncertainty, positioning of experts, and acknowledgment of the potential harms of the measures taken.
- Alex de Waal, [New Pathogen, Old Politics](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/alex-de-waal-thining-critically-pandemic/). *Boston Review*, 2020.
- Trisha Greenhalgh, [Will Evidence-Based Medicine Survive COVID-19?](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/trisha-greenhalgh-covid-19-and-evidence-based-medicine/) *Boston Review*, 2020.
- M. Levinson and D.S. Markovits, [The Biggest Disruption in the History of American Education](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2022/06/covid-learning-loss-remote-school/661360/). *The Atlantic*, June 23, 2022.

## Part 3

- Jonas B. Sandbrink, [Artificial intelligence and biological misuse: Differentiating risks of language models and biological design tools](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13952). arXiv, 2023. Groundbreaking at the time; now seems quaint. Why?
- Excerpts from NAS, [*The Age of AI in the Life Sciences: Benefits and Biosecurity Considerations*](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28868/chapter/1). Out of date and not well written, but may have some good material.
- The rest of Endy et al., [*Biosecurity Really: A Strategy for Victory*](https://www.hoover.org/research/biosecurity-really-strategy-victory) (2026), after Chapter 1.

## Part 4

- Partha Dasgupta and Paul A. David, [Toward a new economics of science](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0048733394010021). *Research Policy* 23(5): 487–521, 1994.
- Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations, [CEPI 3.0 strategy](https://static.cepi.net/downloads/2026-02/CEPI%203.0%20Strategy%20Report%20DIG%20ENG%20FINAL.pdf).
- BBC, [Solving the paradoxical (and brutal) economics of antibiotic resistance](https://www.bbc.com/audio/play/w3ct8js5) (audio).
- Institute for Progress, [Progress deferred: lessons from mRNA vaccine development](https://ifp.org/progress-deferred-lessons-from-mrna-vaccine-development/).
- Athey et al., 2022, on vaccines for pandemics. *Link to be added.*
