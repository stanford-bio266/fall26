# Suggested readings/media for response papers (many of these could be expanded into final projects)

## Part 1:

- Respond to the remainder of Jacobsen, Biological War: A Scenario (parts 3-end).
- Analyze the data provided by CDC during the pandemic – what are strengths and weaknesses of this presentation for normal citizens (use the [Wayback machine](https://web.archive.org/web/20201017044641/https://covid.cdc.gov/covid-data-tracker/#cases_casesinlast7days) from say a date in October 2020 and navigate to [https://covid.cdc.gov/covid-data-tracker/](https://covid.cdc.gov/covid-data-tracker/) archived for that date)
- Respond to one or more posts on [Inspired Air](https://inspiredair.substack.com/)

## Part 1 & 2:

- Assess the potential benefits and limitations for biosecurity of new pathogen-agnostic pharmaceutical interventions that target innate immunity. References [here](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41577-024-00990-1) and [here](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aea1260).

## Part 2:

- Identify media clips (eg videos) of COVID-era pandemic communication from two or more “jurisdictions” (US states, countries, WHO) and compare and contrast the style, the approach to uncertainty, the positioning of experts, and the degree to which they acknowledge the potential harms of the measures taken.
- Respond to Alex De Waal, [New Pathogen, Old Politics](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/alex-de-waal-thining-critically-pandemic/). Boston Review 2020
- Respond to Trisha Greenhalgh, [Will Evidence-based Medicine Survive COVID-19?](https://www.bostonreview.net/articles/trisha-greenhalgh-covid-19-and-evidence-based-medicine/). Boston Review 2020
- Respond to M Levinson and DS Markovits. [The biggest disruption in the history of American Education.](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2022/06/covid-learning-loss-remote-school/661360/) The Atlantic June 23, 2022

## Part 3:

- Respond to: Jonas B. Sandbrink. 2023. [Artificial intelligence and biological misuse: Differentiating risks of language models and biological design tools.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13952) ArXiv preprint. Was groundbreaking at the time and now seems quaint. Why?
- Excerpts from NAS: [The Age of AI in the Life Sciences: Benefits and Biosecurity Considerations](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/28868/chapter/1) Out of date and not well written but may have some good material
- Respond to: The rest of Drew Endy, Sarah Moront, Vassilis Andrea Alexopoulos, Raj Patel, Rhea Jain, Britney Bennett [Biosecurity Really: A Strategy for Victory](https://www.hoover.org/research/biosecurity-really-strategy-victory). 2026. After chapter 1 which we read in class.
