# Assignment 3: Final paper and project.

Length: 3000-4000 words (3 units); 4500-6000 words (4 units) not including references. Oral presentation. This should be a more extended essay that involves reading and using sources that are not part of the assigned reading (this could include unassigned parts of assigned works, or optional readings). The topic is up to you. Some ideas that I think would make good papers/projects are listed below. We have 3, 80 minute classes (240 min) for the final and 16 students enrolled, 5 for 3 units and 11 for 4 units.

Please create the paper as a Google doc and share it with me when you turn it in. I would like to be able to look at your revision history.

This means we will allocate 15 min (including questions) for 4-unit presentations and 10 min (including questions) for 3-unit presentations, with some space for transitions etc.

## Scoring

The scoring framework for this assignment is:

| | |
|:--|:--|
| 10% | Addresses a question that is important as argued in the paper/presentation |
| 15% | Has a clear thesis that is well supported in the body of the paper/presentation |
| 20% | Accurate scientifically and technically, if applicable, and considers relevant facts and arguments including those that are opposed to the author’s conclusion |
| 15% | Uses at least three sources outside required class readings (can be videos, podcasts, personal interviews, web pages) |
| 20% | Writing and presentation are clear and easy to follow. |
| 20% | Answers to questions and discussion in the presentation indicate strong familiarity with the topic. Visual aids in the presentation (if any) are aids and are not a list of points to be read from. |

## Example topics

Example topics I think could make good final papers are below. You are welcome to pick from this list and encouraged to pick your own topic; these are just here to get you thinking.

NOTE: Below the phrase “critical evaluation” does not mean the paper has to conclude against the idea being evaluated, but that it should subject the idea to critical scrutiny and then form a conclusion, which may be that it holds up to scrutiny or that it doesn’t.

1. Critical evaluation of the framing of biosecurity in the US as primarily a contest with China.
2. Critical evaluation of the feasibility/effectiveness of one of the major approaches proposed for limiting AIxBio risks (e.g. gene synthesis screening, model and data access controls).
3. Proposed strategic plan for a funding agency planning $100m (or more) targeted funding to enhance biosecurity in the age of AIxbio
4. Consideration of equity/effectiveness tradeoffs in pandemic vaccine (or other countermeasure) allocation. Why and in what circumstances does such a tradeoff arise? How can it be mitigated?
5. Proposal for how a surveillance system at a national level could be designed to guide better responses to the next pandemic.
6. Detailed proposed guidelines for how to evaluate and improve the benefit-risk profile of research that has biosafety risks (e.g. enhancing transmissibility of viruses).
7. Proposal for a new international organization to address some problem in governance of biorisk – a market failure, a lack of governance, or other missing piece. This should include a rationale for why nations or other parties would want to sign up to it.
8. Assess in detail one or two aspects of the response to COVID-19 or mpox outbreaks in a jurisdiction you are interested in.
