## Requirements

| | 3 units | 4 units |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Length (excluding references) | 3000–4000 words | 4500–6000 words |
| Presentation slot (incl. questions) | 10 min | 15 min |

- A more extended essay that reads and uses **sources outside the assigned reading** (unassigned parts of assigned works and optional readings count).
- The topic is up to you; see [example topics](#example-topics) below.
- By **Mon 19 Oct**, have a conference with Professor Lipsitch and write a 1-paragraph, ungraded plan.
- Write the paper as a **Google Doc** and share it with [lipsitch@stanford.edu](mailto:lipsitch@stanford.edu) when you submit.

## Scoring

| Weight | Criterion |
|:--|:--|
| 10% | Addresses a question that is important, as argued in the paper/presentation |
| 15% | Has a clear thesis that is well supported in the body |
| 20% | Scientifically and technically accurate; considers relevant facts and arguments, including those opposed to your conclusion |
| 15% | Uses **at least three sources** outside required class readings (videos, podcasts, personal interviews, web pages all count) |
| 20% | Writing and presentation are clear and easy to follow |
| 20% | Answers to questions show strong familiarity with the topic; visual aids (if any) are aids, not lists of points to read from |

## Oral presentation

Three 80-minute classes (19 Nov, 1 Dec, 3 Dec) for 16 students: 15 minutes for 4-unit students and 10 minutes for 3-unit students, including questions.

> **Important:** You may not read text from slides. Slides are optional. See the [AI policy](../course-info/ai-policy.md) for what AI may do for slides.

## Example topics

You're encouraged to pick your own. "Critical evaluation" means subjecting an idea to scrutiny and then forming a conclusion, which may be that it holds up.

1. Critical evaluation of the framing of biosecurity in the US as primarily a contest with China.
2. Critical evaluation of the feasibility/effectiveness of one of the major approaches proposed for limiting AIxBio risks (e.g. gene synthesis screening, model and data access controls).
3. A proposed strategic plan for a funding agency planning $100m (or more) of targeted funding to enhance biosecurity in the age of AIxBio.
4. Equity/effectiveness tradeoffs in pandemic vaccine (or other countermeasure) allocation. Why and when does such a tradeoff arise? How can it be mitigated?
5. A proposal for how a national surveillance system could be designed to guide better responses to the next pandemic.
6. Detailed proposed guidelines for evaluating and improving the benefit-risk profile of research with biosafety risks (e.g. enhancing transmissibility of viruses).
7. A proposal for a new international organization to address a problem in biorisk governance (a market failure, a lack of governance, or another missing piece), including why nations or other parties would want to sign up.
8. A detailed assessment of one or two aspects of the response to COVID-19 or mpox outbreaks in a jurisdiction you're interested in.
