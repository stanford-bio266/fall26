# Reaction Papers 1 and 2

Two short essays, 500 to 1000 words each, giving your own response to a reading of your choice. The first is due 13 October and the second 10 November.

## The assignment

Select one, or at most two, readings that were not assigned to you (could be ones assigned to others in the class on divided-reading days), or unassigned parts of readings that we did assign, and write an essay giving your thoughts or personal reaction to the reading. You may choose from the [suggested readings](suggested-readings.md) listed in the syllabus, or something that the syllabus does not mention at all, including a video, podcast, or recent news item.

This is not meant to be a summary. The essay should engage with the reading by taking issue with it , filling out its argument, or using it as a jumping-off point for an idea of your own. You may summarize the part you are debating, filling out, or jumping off from (though not jumping completely off; note below that engagement with the material is key),, but summarizing is not the goal. Cite the reading you use. The point is to get you thinking creatively, critically, or both about the broad themes in the class.

## What I am looking for

Some guidance that past papers suggest is worth stating outright.

- **Disagreeing with me or with assigned readings is fine.** Arguing against a position I stated, or one taken in a reading I assigned, is welcome and costs you nothing. Many of the papers I have most enjoyed are the ones that made me reconsider something.
- **Make personal experience do argumentative work.** Drawing on your own background, community, or research experience is encouraged, and often produces the most interesting papers. But the essay needs to find the general consideration or lesson that follows from the particular experience, rather than leaving the experience to speak for itself.
- **Connect to the course.** Whatever source you choose, including one from well outside the syllabus, bring it into contact with our readings, lectures, or recurring themes from the class.
- **Represent the reading accurately.** Because this is not a summary assignment, it is easy to engage with a source loosely. Misstating what an author actually argues will limit the grade however good the rest of the essay is.
- **Go one step further than feels necessary.** The most common shortfall is a good idea stated and then dropped. Work out the implications, name the obvious obstacles and how they might be addressed, say what would have to be true for your argument to hold.

On length: 500 to 1000 words is a guide, not a limit. Going over is not penalized, but length earns no credit on its own, and a paper in this range should not consume a large share of your week. Most students seem to have spent between 1.5 and 2 hours of active writing/editing time for each paper.

## How the papers are graded

Two criteria matter most, and I will call them the key criteria: your own contribution and accurate, substantive engagement with the reading. Without them the paper won’t receive a good grade. The percentages below are approximate, meant to convey relative emphasis rather than to be added up.

| Criterion | Approx. weight | What earns credit |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Your own contribution (key) | 35% | The essay goes somewhere the reading does not: it takes issue with the argument, supplies a missing piece, or uses it as a launch point. A specific experience or case is turned into a transferable lesson. |
| Engagement with the reading (key) | 20% | The source's argument is represented as the author actually made it, with specific passages, figures, or mechanisms cited rather than gestured at. |
| Argumentative rigor | 20% | Claims are supported rather than asserted. Counterarguments are raised and answered. Comparative claims, such as one risk being larger than another, are actually argued. The essay is internally consistent. |
| Connection to the course | 15% | Draws on class readings, lectures, or the recurring themes of the course, whatever source you have chosen. |
| Development | 10% | The idea is carried through to its implications, obstacles, and conditions. |

A paper that misreads its source, or that never really engages it, will not reach the A range on the strength of the other criteria. Nor will a paper that is accurate and thorough about a reading but adds nothing of the author's own.

A paper is never marked down for reaching a conclusion I disagree with. It is marked down for being easy to agree with because it has not said anything of its own.

Create the paper as a Google doc and share it with me at [lipsitch@stanford.edu](mailto:lipsitch@stanford.edu), in addition to submitting it. I would like to be able to look at your revision history.
