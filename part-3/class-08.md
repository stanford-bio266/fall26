## Learning goals

1. Understand the capabilities of synthetic biology for constructing microbes, toxins, and other biological materials, and how these might be misused.
2. Analyze and evaluate competing arguments about how pressing the threat from AI-enabled biology (especially synthetic biology) is.
3. Apply learnings from earlier classes to assess weak points that might undermine Sherwood-Randall's concept of "deterrence by resilience."
4. Articulate what has and hasn't been done by a landmark paper (King et al.) and assess its implications for biosecurity.

## Before class

### Read

1. **[Synthetic Biology](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues/Synthetic-Biology)**. NHGRI, genome.gov. (2 pages)
2. **[AI and the New Age of Bioweapons: Preparing for a World of Synthetic Pathogens](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/ai-new-age-bioweapons-elizabeth-sherwood-randall)**. Elizabeth Sherwood-Randall. *Foreign Affairs*, Sep/Oct 2026. (about 11 pages)
3. **[The AI Pandemic Isn't On Its Way](https://www.theatlantic.com/health/2026/09/ai-virus-nature-pandemic/688569/)**. Katherine J. Wu. *The Atlantic*, September 2026. (about 3 pages)
4. **[Biosecurity Really: A Strategy for Victory](https://www.hoover.org/research/biosecurity-really-strategy-victory)**, Chapter 1: Worst-Case Scenarios, pp. 3–21. Drew Endy, Sarah Moront, Vassilis Andrea Alexopoulos, Raj Patel, Rhea Jain, Britney Bennett. 2026. (19 pages)
5. **[Generative design of bacteriophages with genome language models](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aec2657)**. King SH, Driscoll CL, Li DB, Guo D, Merchant AT, Brixi G, Wilkinson ME, Hie BL. *Science* 2026;393(6811):eaec2657.
   - Ideally for this class; if not, for the next. Try reading it. If you are not familiar with microbiology or AI models in biology, this is a great candidate for uploading to Claude and asking questions: why does it concern biosecurity experts? What was actually done? What would be required to make this technology create a bioweapon? (It may refuse to answer that for safety reasons.)

### Optional

1. **[Synthetic Biology 2045: What Becomes Possible](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GE0mVfrGyRo&list=PLHIaV3j3LqxY&index=2)**. Jim Collins. National Academy of Medicine keynote, August 2026.
   - Watch up to 21:17, where he moves to antibiotic resistance. The conference was prompted by the King et al. paper above. Fascinating if you know some bioengineering, possibly rapid-fire if you're newer. Key takeaways: synthetic biology allows enormous possibilities for manipulating organisms for good and ill, and AI adds to that power. Collins is one of the world's foremost synthetic biologists.

## In class: discussion

- Terms: synbio, genome editing, generative biology
- What does synbio alone make possible?
- How does AI amplify that?
- What other technologies besides synbio and AI are enabling?
- "Most scientists, ethicists and policymakers agree that entire societies must discuss and weigh the potential harms and benefits of synthetic biology in order to answer these questions."
- Sherwood-Randall vs. Wu: their views, their differences, their common points. Anything they miss? Evolution? Why might they see it so differently?
