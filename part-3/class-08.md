## Class 8 learning goals

1. To understand the capabilities of synthetic biology for constructing microbes, toxins, and other biological materials, and how this might be misused
2. To analyze and evaluate competing arguments about how pressing the threat from AI-enabled biology (especially synthetic biology) is.
3. To apply learnings from earlier classes to assess weak points that might undermine Sherwood-Randall’s concept of “deterrence by resilience”
4. To articulate what has and hasn’t been done by a landmark paper (King et al) and assess its implications for biosecurity.

## Class 8 advance preparation

1. Read [https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues/Synthetic-Biology](https://www.genome.gov/about-genomics/policy-issues/Synthetic-Biology) 2 pages
2. Read Elizabeth Sherwood-Randall. **[AI and the New Age of Bioweapons: Preparing for a World of Synthetic Pathogens](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/ai-new-age-bioweapons-elizabeth-sherwood-randall)**. Foreign Affairs, Sep/Oct 2026. (Approx 11 pages)
3. Read Katherine J. Wu. September 2026. **[The AI Pandemic Isn’t On Its Way.](https://www.theatlantic.com/health/2026/09/ai-virus-nature-pandemic/688569/)** The Atlantic. (~3 pages)
4. Read Drew Endy, Sarah Moront, Vassilis Andrea Alexopoulos, Raj Patel, Rhea Jain, Britney Bennett **[Biosecurity Really: A Strategy for Victory](https://www.hoover.org/research/biosecurity-really-strategy-victory)**. 2026. Chapter 1: Worst-Case Scenarios, pp. 3-21. (19 pages)
5. Read (for this class ideally; if not then for the next) King SH, Driscoll CL, Li DB, Guo D, Merchant AT, Brixi G, Wilkinson ME, Hie BL. **[Generative design of bacteriophages with genome language models](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aec2657)**. Science. 2026;393(6811):eaec2657. doi: doi:10.1126/science.aec2657.
   - Try reading this paper. If you are not familiar with microbiology or with AI models in biology, this is a great candidate for uploading to Claude and asking questions about it – why does it concern biosecurity experts? What was actually done? What would be required to make this technology create a bioweapon (it may refuse to answer this for safety reasons)?
6. Optional to post on Canvas – this is a lot of reading. If you do, choose the topic; the reading should be thought-provoking.

OPTIONAL: Watch Jim Collins **[Synthetic Biology 2045: what becomes possible.](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GE0mVfrGyRo&list=PLHIaV3j3LqxY&index=2)** National Academy of Medicine, August 2026 Keynote. Up to 21:17 where he talks about antibiotic resistance, a different topic. Note: The conference where this was the keynote talk was prompted by the paper in #5 above. This will be fascinating for those with some knowledge of bioengineering, and may be a bit rapid-fire for those newer to the subject. Key take-aways are that synthetic biology allows enormous possibilities for manipulating organisms for good and ill, and that AI adds to the power of these possibilities. Collins is one of the world’s foremost synthetic biologists and worth hearing even if you don’t get every minute.

## Class 8 agenda

Discussion

- Terms: synbio, genome editing, generative biology
- What does synbio alone make possible?
- How does AI amplify that?
- What are the other technologies besides synbio and AI that enable?
- “Most scientists, ethicists and policymakers agree that entire societies must discuss and weigh the potential harms and benefits of synthetic biology in order to answer these questions.”
- Sherwood-Randall vs. Wu
  - Their views
  - Their differences
  - Their common points
  - Anything they miss? Evolution?
  - Why might they see it so differently?
